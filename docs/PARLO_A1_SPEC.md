# Parlo A1 Curriculum and Learning System Specification

**Status:** Product specification for approval  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Scope:** Complete CEFR A1 French learning experience  
**Product principle:** Parlo should always know what the learner knows, what they do not know, and what they should learn next.

## 1. Purpose

Parlo A1 will take a learner with no French, or very limited French, to functional A1 ability through a coherent course rather than a collection of reference pages. The experience must combine instruction, guided practice, recall, real-world production, assessment, review, and personalized next-step recommendations.

An A1 graduate should be able to understand and use familiar everyday expressions, introduce themselves and others, ask and answer basic personal questions, and interact simply when the other person speaks clearly and cooperatively.

Parlo A1 is the foundation for later A2–B2 and TEF Canada tracks. A1 must not teach to the TEF format prematurely, but it should build the language abilities that later exam preparation requires.

## 2. Target learner

Primary learner:

- Adult or older teen beginning French.
- Uses English as the initial interface/support language.
- Studies independently for 15–60 minutes per day.
- Wants practical general French, with an optional long-term TEF Canada goal.
- May have uneven prior knowledge and may be stronger in reading than listening or speaking.

The experience must also support:

- True beginners who need pronunciation and literacy foundations.
- Returning beginners who can test out of familiar material.
- Learners interested in either general French or Canadian French.
- Mobile, keyboard-only, and assistive-technology users.

## 3. A1 completion definition

A learner completes Parlo A1 when all of the following are true:

1. All required unit capability assessments are passed.
2. The final A1 assessment is passed overall.
3. No core skill is below its minimum threshold.
4. Required vocabulary and grammar concepts meet the retention threshold.
5. Required speaking and writing tasks have been attempted and meet the A1 rubric, either through supported automated feedback or self/teacher review.

Completion must represent demonstrated ability, not merely opening lessons or accumulating points.

Suggested initial thresholds, subject to calibration with real learner data:

- Overall final assessment: 70%.
- Reading and listening: at least 65% each.
- Vocabulary and grammar: at least 70% each.
- Speaking and writing rubric: at least 2 on a 0–3 A1 rubric.
- Core memory items: at least 80% classified as Familiar or stronger.

Learners may continue studying after a first failed assessment. Parlo should create a remediation plan based on observed weaknesses rather than reset the course.

## 4. Learning design principles

1. **Capability first:** Every lesson supports a real “I can…” outcome.
2. **Context before abstraction:** Introduce language through a dialogue, image, situation, or short text before formal explanation.
3. **Recognition to production:** Activities progress from noticing and recognition to recall and independent use.
4. **Audio from the beginning:** Learners hear French before and while reading it.
5. **Short explanations, repeated application:** Grammar supports communication and is revisited across units.
6. **Phrases as well as words:** Teach useful chunks, collocations, and sentence frames.
7. **Retrieval over rereading:** Learners must recall and use material, not only browse it.
8. **Mistakes are curriculum input:** Errors create targeted review opportunities.
9. **Mastery decays:** Previously correct material still requires spaced review.
10. **Transparent adaptation:** Parlo explains why it recommends an activity.
11. **Multiple French varieties:** Core French is shared; relevant France/Canada differences are labeled without presenting one variety as defective.
12. **Human-reviewed content:** Generated content is never published without linguistic and pedagogical review.

## 5. Course structure

```text
Course
└── CEFR level
    └── Unit
        └── Lesson
            └── Activity
                └── Item/attempt
```

The required A1 course contains 12 units. Each unit contains approximately 5 core lessons, one integrated review, and one capability assessment. Exact lesson length may vary, but a core lesson should normally take 10–20 minutes.

### Unit 1 — French sounds and first contact

**Capability:** I can recognize key French sounds, greet someone, and use basic classroom survival expressions.

Core lessons:

1. Greetings and farewells.
2. French alphabet and spelling a name.
3. Core sound contrasts: `u/ou`, `é/è`, nasal vowels, and French `r` awareness.
4. Polite expressions: please, thank you, sorry, and excuse me.
5. Classroom phrases: repeat, slower, I understand, and I do not understand.

Language coverage:

- `bonjour`, `bonsoir`, `salut`, `au revoir`, `merci`, `s'il vous plaît`.
- Subject pronouns as recognition only.
- `tu` versus `vous` introduction.
- Basic punctuation, apostrophes, accents, and French spelling conventions.

Assessment task: greet a person, spell a name, select or produce an appropriate polite expression, and discriminate key sounds.

### Unit 2 — Introductions and identity

**Capability:** I can introduce myself and ask who someone is.

Core lessons:

1. Saying and asking names.
2. `être` in the present.
3. Nationalities and countries.
4. Professions and basic identity descriptions.
5. Formal and informal introductions.

Language coverage:

- `Je m'appelle…`, `Comment tu t'appelles ?`, `Comment vous appelez-vous ?`.
- `être`; subject pronouns.
- Gender and number agreement with common nationality/profession adjectives.
- Basic statement and question intonation.

Assessment task: complete a short spoken or written self-introduction and understand another person's introduction.

### Unit 3 — Personal information

**Capability:** I can exchange basic personal information.

Core lessons:

1. Numbers 0–69.
2. Age and `avoir`.
3. Addresses, phone numbers, and email addresses.
4. Countries, cities, and `habiter/venir`.
5. Asking basic information questions.

Language coverage:

- `avoir`; regular `-er` verb introduction.
- `à`, `en`, `au`, `aux`, and `de/du/des` in common country/city frames.
- Question words: `qui`, `où`, `comment`, `quel`, `combien`.

Assessment task: complete and conduct a basic registration exchange.

### Unit 4 — Family and people

**Capability:** I can describe my family and a person simply.

Core lessons:

1. Family members.
2. Possessive adjectives: `mon/ma/mes`, `ton/ta/tes`, `son/sa/ses`.
3. Physical and personality descriptions.
4. Adjective agreement and common adjective position.
5. `c'est`, `ce sont`, and `il/elle est`.

Language coverage:

- Family vocabulary and relationships.
- Gender, plural formation, and adjective agreement.
- Common descriptive adjectives.

Assessment task: understand a simple family description and present two or three people.

### Unit 5 — Daily life

**Capability:** I can describe a simple daily routine and say when things happen.

Core lessons:

1. Daily routine verbs.
2. Present tense of common regular `-er` verbs.
3. Reflexive verbs in frequent routine phrases.
4. Telling time and parts of the day.
5. Frequency and basic sequencing.

Language coverage:

- `se lever`, `travailler`, `étudier`, `manger`, `se coucher`.
- `à` + time; `le matin`, `l'après-midi`, `le soir`.
- `toujours`, `souvent`, `parfois`, `rarement`, `jamais`.
- `d'abord`, `ensuite`, `puis`, `enfin`.

Assessment task: follow and describe a short daily schedule.

### Unit 6 — Food and cafés

**Capability:** I can talk about basic food preferences and order in a café or restaurant.

Core lessons:

1. Common foods and drinks.
2. Definite and indefinite articles.
3. Partitive articles and basic quantities.
4. Likes and dislikes with `aimer`, `adorer`, and `détester`.
5. Ordering politely with `je voudrais`.

Language coverage:

- Food, menu, price, and meal vocabulary.
- `le/la/les`, `un/une/des`, `du/de la/de l'`.
- `prendre`, `boire`, and `vouloir` in high-frequency forms.

Assessment task: understand a short order and complete a café role-play.

### Unit 7 — Home and neighborhood

**Capability:** I can describe where I live and locate common places and objects.

Core lessons:

1. Rooms, furniture, and housing.
2. `il y a` and `c'est`.
3. Prepositions of place.
4. Places in town.
5. Asking and giving simple directions.

Language coverage:

- `dans`, `sur`, `sous`, `devant`, `derrière`, `à côté de`, `près de`.
- `aller`; common direction imperatives as useful chunks.
- `à gauche`, `à droite`, `tout droit`.

Assessment task: describe a room and navigate a simple map exchange.

### Unit 8 — Dates, schedules, and plans

**Capability:** I can understand dates and arrange a simple meeting or activity.

Core lessons:

1. Days, months, seasons, and dates.
2. Numbers 70–1,000.
3. Talking about schedules.
4. Invitations and accepting or declining.
5. Near future with `aller + infinitive`.

Language coverage:

- Calendar and appointment vocabulary.
- `quand`, `à quelle heure`, and date formats.
- `On va…`, `Tu veux… ?`, `Je peux/je ne peux pas`.

Assessment task: interpret a simple schedule and arrange an activity.

### Unit 9 — Shopping and clothing

**Capability:** I can ask for an item, understand a price, and make a simple purchase.

Core lessons:

1. Clothing, colors, and sizes.
2. Demonstratives: `ce/cet/cette/ces`.
3. Asking about price and quantity.
4. Trying, choosing, and buying.
5. Basic comparisons.

Language coverage:

- `combien coûte… ?`, `je cherche…`, `je prends…`.
- Color agreement.
- `plus/moins/aussi … que` at an introductory level.

Assessment task: understand product details and complete a shop dialogue.

### Unit 10 — Travel and transport

**Capability:** I can use basic transport information and handle a simple trip.

Core lessons:

1. Transport and destinations.
2. Tickets, departures, and arrivals.
3. `aller`, `venir`, and `prendre` in useful present forms.
4. Weather and packing basics.
5. Asking for clarification during travel.

Language coverage:

- Stations, stops, platforms, tickets, and basic travel times.
- `pour`, `vers`, `de… à…`.
- Weather frames with `il fait` and `il y a`.

Assessment task: extract information from a timetable and buy or request a ticket.

### Unit 11 — Health and immediate needs

**Capability:** I can state a basic health problem, ask for help, and understand simple advice.

Core lessons:

1. Body parts and common symptoms.
2. `avoir mal à` and basic health expressions.
3. Pharmacy and doctor interactions.
4. Obligation and advice with `il faut`, `devoir`, and common imperatives.
5. Emergencies and essential contact information.

Language coverage:

- `J'ai mal…`, `Je suis malade`, `J'ai besoin de…`.
- High-frequency health, medication, and emergency vocabulary.
- Negative statements and basic instructions.

Assessment task: explain a simple problem and identify appropriate advice or action.

### Unit 12 — Experiences, review, and A1 integration

**Capability:** I can combine A1 language to manage a short everyday interaction and describe a recent simple event.

Core lessons:

1. High-frequency review based on learner weaknesses.
2. Introduction to the `passé composé` with common `avoir` verbs.
3. Essential `être` movement forms as fixed high-frequency patterns.
4. Integrated reading and listening scenarios.
5. Guided speaking and writing portfolio task.

Language coverage:

- A restrained introduction to recent completed events.
- Review of questions, negation, present tense, near future, articles, agreement, time, and place.

Assessment task: complete the A1 capstone scenario and become eligible for the final A1 assessment.

## 6. Cross-course coverage targets

Final numerical targets should be validated during content production. The initial planning targets are:

- 750–1,000 actively taught lexical items, including multiword expressions.
- At least two contextual examples for each core lexical item.
- 45–60 communicative or grammar concepts.
- 60–90 short dialogues/readings across the course.
- At least 300 reviewed assessment items, excluding automatically generated variants.
- At least 24 guided speaking tasks and 24 guided writing tasks.
- Listening exposure from multiple speakers at learner and natural speeds.

Content counts are quality safeguards, not learner-facing promises. Coverage and usefulness take precedence over reaching an arbitrary number.

### Required grammar coverage

- Subject pronouns.
- `être`, `avoir`, `aller`, `faire`, and other common irregular verbs in useful present forms.
- Present tense of common regular verbs.
- Introductory reflexive verbs.
- Definite, indefinite, and partitive articles.
- Noun gender and plural.
- Adjective gender, number, and common placement.
- Possessive and demonstrative adjectives.
- `c'est`, `ce sont`, and `il y a`.
- Basic negation.
- Intonation, `est-ce que`, and common question words.
- Prepositions of place, city, country, origin, destination, and time.
- Telling time and expressing dates.
- Basic quantity and comparison.
- Near future.
- High-frequency imperative forms.
- Introductory recent-event `passé composé` appropriate to A1 use.

### Required pronunciation coverage

- Alphabet and spelling.
- French rhythm and syllable timing.
- Silent final consonant awareness.
- Elision.
- Basic liaison awareness in frequent phrases.
- `u` versus `ou`.
- `é` versus `è`.
- Nasal vowels: `an/en`, `on`, and `in/ain`.
- French `r` awareness without demanding native imitation.
- Common sound-spelling correspondences.
- Question versus statement intonation.

## 7. Standard lesson anatomy

Every core lesson follows this learning sequence:

1. **Objective:** A learner-facing “I can…” statement and estimated time.
2. **Warm retrieval:** One to three questions from prerequisite or due material.
3. **Context:** A short dialogue, scene, audio clip, or reading containing the target language.
4. **Notice:** Guided attention to meaning, form, sound, or usage.
5. **Teach:** A concise explanation with examples and common mistakes.
6. **Guided practice:** Recognition and constrained production.
7. **Independent use:** Typing, speaking, listening, or sentence creation without immediate scaffolding.
8. **Feedback:** Correct answer, explanation, and a focused retry when useful.
9. **Exit check:** A small unseen check of the lesson objective.
10. **Reflection:** Confidence prompt and a summary of what entered review.

A lesson is passed when the exit check meets its threshold. A failed exit check does not block the learner indefinitely; it produces remediation and a later retry.

## 8. Activity catalog

### Foundation activities

Required for the first complete A1 release:

- Learn card with audio and contextual example.
- Meaning selection.
- Audio-to-text selection.
- Text-to-audio selection.
- Fill in the blank.
- Sentence builder.
- Typed word or phrase recall.
- Dictation.
- Matching.
- Grammar/form selection.
- Reading comprehension.
- Listening comprehension.
- Guided writing prompt.
- Guided speaking/record-and-playback prompt.

### Intelligent-learning activities

Required after the foundation is stable:

- Adaptive spaced-review session.
- Mistake-focused mini lesson.
- Confidence-rated recall.
- Variable-speed listening.
- Audio-only comprehension.
- Structured role-play with deterministic branches.
- Personalized mixed-skill review.

### AI-assisted activities

Later A1 enhancement:

- Scenario-based AI conversation.
- Pronunciation feedback at phrase/word level.
- Writing feedback with A1-appropriate explanations.
- “Explain this sentence.”
- AI-generated practice constrained to approved concepts and vocabulary.

AI must not be required to complete the core A1 course. A deterministic fallback must exist for essential learning and assessment.

## 9. Feedback requirements

Every scored activity must provide more than red/green correctness when an explanation is pedagogically useful.

Feedback may include:

- Correct answer.
- Brief reason.
- Contrast with the learner's answer.
- Relevant rule or example.
- Replayable audio.
- One immediate focused retry.
- Link to the prerequisite concept when the error is foundational.

Parlo should distinguish:

- Typographical slips.
- Vocabulary recall errors.
- Gender/article errors.
- Conjugation errors.
- Word-order errors.
- Spelling/accent errors.
- Listening discrimination errors.
- Pronunciation targets.
- Possible misconceptions.

Feedback language must remain encouraging, direct, and adult. It should not over-celebrate trivial actions or shame repeated mistakes.

## 10. Learner and mastery model

Parlo tracks mastery at the concept/item level and summarizes it at lesson, unit, capability, skill, and CEFR levels.

Core learner records:

Each attempt should be capable of recording:

- Learner and content identifiers.
- Prompt and activity version.
- Response and accepted-answer match.
- Correctness or rubric result.
- Response time.
- Hint/replay usage.
- Confidence, when requested.
- Skill and concept tags.
- Error classification.
- Timestamp and session.

### Mastery states

Learner-facing states:

- New.
- Learning.
- Familiar.
- Strong.
- Mastered.
- Needs review.

Internally, mastery should retain a score and confidence/uncertainty rather than store only a label. Mastery rises more from unsupported production than from recognition, and more from delayed success than immediate repetition.

Suggested evidence weighting:

- Passive exposure: no mastery credit.
- Recognition with support: low credit.
- Recognition without support: moderate credit.
- Typed or spoken recall: high credit.
- Use in a new context: highest credit.
- Hints, repeated replay, and very fast guessing reduce confidence in the evidence.

The exact mastery formula must be configurable and tested against learner outcomes. It must not be presented as scientifically precise before validation.

## 11. Spaced review

Spaced repetition operates across vocabulary, phrases, grammar concepts, sound distinctions, and recurring mistakes. It is not confined to a flashcard page.

Every reviewable item maintains at least:

- State/strength.
- Last reviewed time.
- Next due time.
- Successful and failed review counts.
- Recent response quality.
- Recent response time.
- Lapse count.
- Modality history, such as reading, listening, typing, or speaking.

Scheduling rules:

1. New material enters review only after meaningful instruction.
2. Incorrect items return sooner but do not repeat endlessly in one session.
3. Correct unsupported recall lengthens the interval.
4. A lapse after a long interval reduces strength without erasing all history.
5. Reviews vary modality so visual recognition does not masquerade as listening or speaking ability.
6. The daily load is capped according to the learner's available time.
7. Core overdue reviews normally precede new material, but learners retain an explicit override.

Initial implementation may use transparent, configurable interval rules. A more sophisticated memory model should be introduced only when enough attempt data exists to evaluate it.

## 12. Mistake notebook

Relevant mistakes are saved automatically. The learner can also dismiss an incorrectly classified mistake or bookmark an item manually.

Each mistake record includes:

- Original prompt/context.
- Learner response.
- Correct or improved response.
- Explanation.
- Error category.
- Related concepts.
- Occurrence and recurrence count.
- Resolution status.
- Next targeted review.

The notebook groups errors by skill and concept. A mistake is considered resolved only after successful later use, preferably in a different context.

## 13. Today plan and recommendations

The Today experience is the default signed-in home screen. It removes decision fatigue while keeping the full course map accessible.

Inputs:

- Current unit and prerequisites.
- Reviews due.
- Recent mistakes.
- Mastery and uncertainty.
- Skill balance.
- Learner goal and available time.
- Recent workload and unfinished activities.

Typical ordering:

1. Short warm-up.
2. High-priority due review.
3. One primary new lesson.
4. A complementary listening or production activity.
5. Exit check or short reflection.

Every recommendation includes a reason, such as “Review `avoir` because it caused two recent errors.” Time-to-completion estimates must be described as estimates and must adapt to actual pace.

The first recommendation engine should be deterministic and inspectable. AI may explain or enrich recommendations later but should not secretly control progression.

## 14. Course progression

- Units follow the defined sequence because concepts build on earlier concepts.
- Learners may preview future units.
- A learner may test out of lessons or units through a capability check.
- Passing a lesson requires its exit check, not viewing every screen.
- Unit completion requires core lessons and the unit capability assessment.
- Weak prerequisites produce recommended remediation rather than a hard dead end.
- Previously completed units may return to “Needs review” without revoking completion.
- Optional enrichment never blocks progression.

Progress must distinguish:

- Viewed.
- Attempted.
- Completed.
- Currently retained.
- Demonstrated in an assessment.

## 15. Assessment design

### Lesson exit checks

- Two to five unseen items.
- Directly measure the stated objective.
- Include production when the objective requires production.
- Permit a later alternate-form retry.

### Unit capability assessments

- Integrated real-world scenario.
- Cover relevant reading, listening, speaking, and writing at an appropriate scale.
- Include vocabulary and grammar inside communication rather than only as isolated questions.
- Use unseen or meaningfully varied items.
- Return a capability result, skill breakdown, and remediation plan.

### Final A1 assessment

Required sections:

1. Listening: short announcements, exchanges, numbers, times, and everyday requests.
2. Reading: notices, messages, forms, menus, schedules, and short personal texts.
3. Writing: a form plus a short message or self-description.
4. Speaking: introduction, personal questions, and a short everyday role-play.
5. Language knowledge: diagnostic vocabulary/grammar sampling, not the sole basis for completion.

Security is not the primary goal of the learning assessment, but item pools and alternate forms should discourage memorizing a single answer set.

Automated speaking/writing results must be labeled as estimates until validated. Learners must be able to review the rubric and evidence behind the result.

## 16. A1 production rubrics

Use a simple 0–3 scale for guided speaking and writing:

- **0 — Not demonstrated:** Response absent or meaning cannot be understood.
- **1 — Emerging:** Communicates fragments with substantial support; frequent problems obscure meaning.
- **2 — A1 functional:** Communicates the required basic meaning with simple language; errors occur but meaning is generally clear.
- **3 — Secure for task:** Completes the A1 task clearly with appropriate familiar language and limited support.

Rubric dimensions:

- Task completion.
- Comprehensibility.
- Relevant vocabulary.
- Control of practiced structures.
- For speaking: intelligibility, rhythm, and interaction.
- For writing: basic spelling, punctuation, and organization.

Accent must never be treated as an error when speech is intelligible.

The graph supports:

## 19. Audio, listening, and pronunciation

- All core French vocabulary, phrases, dialogues, prompts, and listening assessments require reviewed audio.
- Dialogues should use more than one speaker.
- Learner material may offer slower playback; assessment material follows its defined conditions.
- Text transcripts may be hidden initially and revealed as support.
- Audio metadata records speaker, locale, speed, source, license/provenance, and text version.
- French from France and Canadian French differences are labeled where relevant.
- Generated audio must undergo sampling and exception review for names, numbers, liaisons, acronyms, and ambiguous spellings.

Pronunciation feedback initially consists of models, articulatory guidance, recording, playback, and self-comparison. Automated scoring is a later enhancement and must communicate uncertainty.

## 20. AI feature requirements

AI is used where variability and personalized feedback materially improve learning. It must remain constrained by the learner's approved A1 vocabulary, concepts, and scenario.

AI conversation must know:

- Current unit and A1 boundaries.
- Scenario and role.
- Learner's practiced concepts.
- Relevant recurring mistakes.
- Desired conversation length and support level.

After a conversation it should provide:

- What the learner communicated successfully.
- A small number of high-value corrections.
- Improved versions and explanations.
- A focused retry.
- Proposed mistake records for learner confirmation or automatic saving under clear settings.

Safety and quality requirements:

- Do not claim official CEFR, TEF, or clinical speech evaluation.
- Do not expose private learner data in prompts unnecessarily.
- Do not publish learner conversations by default.
- Provide deletion/export controls.
- Filter unsafe content while avoiding inappropriate infantilization.
- Log model/version and prompt-policy version for evaluated work.
- Provide deterministic lesson alternatives when AI is unavailable.

## 21. Onboarding and placement

Onboarding asks only what is necessary to create a useful path:

- General French or future TEF goal.
- Prior experience.
- Preferred interface language.
- Preferred French variety, if any.
- Daily/weekly study target.
- Optional target date.
- Accessibility and audio preferences.

The optional placement experience samples vocabulary, grammar, reading, and listening, followed by a short speaking/writing self-check or supported task. It recommends a start point but does not prevent the learner from choosing another unit.

True beginners can skip placement and begin Unit 1 immediately.

## 23. Accessibility and inclusion

Parlo targets WCAG 2.2 AA for the core learning experience.

Requirements include:

- Complete keyboard operation.
- Visible focus indicators.
- Correct headings, landmarks, labels, and control semantics.
- Focus management and announcements after navigation and dynamic feedback.
- Captions/transcripts for learning media.
- Information never conveyed through color alone.
- Sufficient contrast and scalable text.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Adequate touch targets.
- No forced time limits outside clearly identified assessment tasks.
- Adjustable playback and replay during learning.
- Accessible alternatives for drag-and-drop, recording, and visual-only exercises.
- Plain, respectful language suitable for adults.

Accessibility must be tested with automated tools, keyboard review, and representative screen-reader flows.

## 24. Privacy and learner control

- Collect the minimum data necessary for learning and product operation.
- Clearly separate required operational data from optional analytics.
- Provide progress export and account deletion.
- Treat voice recordings, written responses, and AI conversations as private learner data.
- Define retention periods and deletion behavior.
- Do not use learner content for public examples or model training without explicit informed consent.
- Support a local/guest experience where practical, with clear limitations.
- Never commit private ChatGPT exports or personal learning history to the public repository.

## 25. Content quality workflow

Publishing states:

```text
Draft → Linguistic review → Pedagogical review → Media/answer validation → Published
```

Required automated checks:

- Unique stable IDs.
- Valid references and prerequisite links.
- Answers compatible with the response format.
- No duplicate or empty required fields.
- Audio/media existence and version match.
- Locale and CEFR metadata present.
- Accepted-answer normalization tests.
- No unresolved draft content in a release.
- Assessment items not accidentally exposed as practice answer keys.

ChatGPT exports or other AI-generated materials may enter only as drafts. Personal information must be removed before material enters the content workflow.

## 26. Measurement and product analytics

Measure learning and product health separately.

Learning measures:

- Delayed retention.
- Capability-assessment results.
- Skill balance.
- Error recurrence and resolution.
- Review completion and backlog.
- Time to demonstrated mastery.

Product measures:

- Onboarding completion.
- Lesson and unit continuation.
- Return rate.
- Session completion.
- Exercise abandonment.
- Audio, recording, and AI failure rates.

Streaks and points may motivate learners but must not substitute for learning measures. Analytics must respect privacy settings and should not encourage unhealthy study behavior.

## 27. Delivery phases

### Phase 0 — Approved foundation design

- Approve this product/curriculum specification.
- Create detailed lesson inventory and coverage matrix.
- Approve the content schema and stable-ID convention.
- Choose persistence, authentication, routing, and testing architecture.
- Define design system and accessibility baseline.

### Phase 1 — A1 learning foundation

- Implement routes and course/lesson structure.
- Implement learner progress, attempts, and local or account persistence.
- Build the foundation activity catalog.
- Produce and review Units 1–3 as a vertical slice.
- Add deterministic review scheduling.
- Add mistake capture and basic Today plan.
- Validate with representative learners before producing the entire course.

### Phase 2 — Complete deterministic A1

- Produce and review Units 4–12.
- Add all unit assessments and the final A1 assessment.
- Complete the vocabulary, grammar, dialogue, reading, listening, writing, and speaking coverage.
- Add capability dashboard, placement, remediation, and mature spaced review.
- Meet accessibility, quality, reliability, and privacy release gates.

### Phase 3 — Intelligent A1

- Improve scheduling using observed outcomes.
- Add adaptive daily planning and prerequisite diagnosis.
- Expand listening modes, structured conversations, and pronunciation lab.
- Calibrate mastery and assessment thresholds.

### Phase 4 — AI-enhanced A1

- Scenario AI tutor.
- Constrained writing feedback.
- Pronunciation feedback.
- Explain-this-sentence feature.
- Personalized coach explanations and practice variants.

Work on A2 should begin only after the A1 content model and learning telemetry have proven reusable. Dedicated TEF task training belongs after sufficient general language ability, though a learner may declare TEF as a long-term goal from onboarding.

## 28. Foundation release acceptance criteria

The deterministic A1 foundation is release-ready when:

- All 12 units and required lessons are published and reviewed.
- Every unit has an integrated capability assessment.
- The final assessment covers listening, reading, writing, and speaking.
- Progress survives reload and synchronizes for authenticated learners if accounts are included in the release.
- Every taught core item can enter and leave the review system correctly.
- Mistakes can be captured, reviewed, and resolved.
- Today recommendations are explainable and respect the learner's time target.
- No required course completion depends on AI availability.
- All core audio and transcripts are present and verified.
- Automated content validation passes.
- Critical learner flows have automated tests.
- Core flows meet the accessibility target.
- Privacy, export, and deletion behavior is documented and tested.
- Content claims accurately describe what the product currently provides.

## 29. Explicit non-goals for the A1 foundation

- A2–B2 course production.
- Full TEF mock examinations or official score prediction.
- Social feeds or learner-to-learner messaging.
- Competitive leagues.
- Teacher marketplace or live tutoring.
- Certificates implying external accreditation.
- Unreviewed infinite AI-generated lessons.
- Native-like accent as a completion requirement.
- A complex machine-learning recommendation model before sufficient data exists.

These may become later product initiatives but must not delay a coherent and effective A1 course.

## 30. Decisions requiring approval before implementation

1. Is the initial audience specifically English-speaking adults, or should multilingual interface support be foundational?
2. Will the first release require accounts, or begin with local progress plus later migration?
3. Will both France and Canadian French audio be produced at launch, or will one be primary with variant notes?
4. What amount of reviewed content can be produced per unit before engineering becomes the bottleneck?
5. Will speaking/writing assessments initially use self-assessment, human review, constrained automation, or a combination?
6. Which capabilities are free, paid, or deferred? This affects AI, audio, storage, and account architecture.
7. What learner research and pilot group will be used to calibrate lesson length, mastery, and assessment thresholds?

## 31. Immediate next deliverables

After this specification is approved, the next non-code artifacts are:

1. **A1 curriculum coverage matrix:** every lesson mapped to objectives, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, skills, prerequisites, activities, and assessment evidence.
2. **Content schema specification:** exact required and optional fields, ID conventions, versions, validation rules, and examples.
3. **Learner-state specification:** attempts, mastery, memory, mistakes, progress, and recommendation inputs/outputs.
4. **Unit 1 production packet:** finalized scripts, vocabulary, explanations, dialogues, activities, answer rationales, audio manifest, and assessment.
5. **V2 implementation architecture:** routing, storage, authentication decision, testing, migration from existing content, and staged rollout.

The first coded milestone should be a vertical slice of Units 1–3 through the complete learning loop—not an empty framework for all future features.
