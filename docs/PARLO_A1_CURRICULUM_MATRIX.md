# Parlo A1 Curriculum Coverage Matrix

**Status:** Curriculum planning artifact for approval  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Parent specification:** [PARLO_A1_SPEC.md](./PARLO_A1_SPEC.md)  
**Scope:** 12 units, 60 core lessons, 12 integrated reviews, and 12 capability assessments

## 1. How to read the matrix

Each unit contains five core lessons, one review, and one capability assessment. The rows define curriculum obligations; they are not yet finished lesson scripts.

### Skill codes

- **L:** Listening
- **S:** Speaking
- **R:** Reading
- **W:** Writing
- **V:** Vocabulary
- **G:** Grammar/form
- **P:** Pronunciation

### Activity codes

- **LC:** Learn/context card
- **MS:** Meaning selection
- **AT:** Audio-to-text selection
- **TA:** Text-to-audio selection
- **FB:** Fill in the blank
- **SB:** Sentence builder
- **TR:** Typed recall
- **DI:** Dictation
- **MA:** Matching
- **GF:** Grammar/form selection
- **RC:** Reading comprehension
- **AC:** Listening comprehension
- **GW:** Guided writing
- **GS:** Guided speaking/record and playback
- **RP:** Structured role-play
- **MR:** Mixed review

### Evidence levels

- **Recognize:** Identify meaning, sound, form, or an appropriate response.
- **Recall:** Produce a word, phrase, form, or sentence with limited scaffolding.
- **Transfer:** Use learned language in a new or integrated context.

Lessons normally include several low-stakes checks. The **Evidence** column identifies the strongest evidence required to pass the lesson exit check.

## 16. Skill-distribution requirements

Each unit must include all four communicative skills, even when one skill is emphasized.

Minimum per unit before its assessment:

- Two listening-comprehension activities using connected language.
- One dictation or focused listening-recall activity.
- Two speaking opportunities, including one multi-turn or extended task.
- Two short readings, at least one representing an authentic everyday text type.
- One meaningful writing task beyond isolated blanks.
- One integrated review using at least three modalities.

Course-wide safeguards:

- No unit may be passed entirely through multiple choice.
- At least half of exit checks require recall rather than recognition.
- Every unit assessment requires productive evidence.
- Listening materials must include multiple reviewed speakers over the course.
- Speaking evaluation prioritizes intelligibility and task completion, not native accent.

## 17. Vocabulary allocation rules

The target of 750–1,000 lexical items includes individual words and high-value multiword expressions. Allocation should approximately follow:

| Category | Planning range | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Core function words and communicative chunks | 120–160 | Questions, politeness, repair, linking, time/place frames. |
| People, identity, and family | 80–110 | Includes professions, nationalities, and description. |
| Daily life, time, dates, and leisure | 120–150 | High-frequency verbs and routine collocations. |
| Food and service interactions | 90–120 | Teach useful menu/order chunks, not exhaustive food lists. |
| Home, town, directions, and transport | 120–150 | Includes spatial and journey phrases. |
| Shopping, clothing, colors, and quantities | 80–110 | Reuses numbers and agreement. |
| Health, weather, and immediate needs | 70–100 | Safety-focused and high frequency. |
| Cross-unit descriptive and grammatical vocabulary | 70–100 | Adjectives, adverbs, connectors, and reusable verbs. |

Rules:

- Count a lemma once even when it appears across units; track each sense separately when necessary.
- Introduce no more new lexical material in a lesson than its activities can retrieve meaningfully.
- Teach nouns with articles and useful plural information.
- Teach verbs through high-frequency frames and collocations, not infinitives alone.
- Every core item needs contextual examples and reviewed audio.
- Every item must have a rationale: frequency, communicative need, prerequisite value, or scenario coverage.

## 20. Decisions and open calibration items

These decisions do not prevent the matrix from guiding architecture, but they must be resolved during detailed content design:

1. Select the exact lexical inventory and document its corpus/communicative rationale.
2. Choose the primary launch audio variety and how Canadian/France variants appear.
3. Confirm whether introductory `passé composé` remains in A1 core or is treated as A1+ enrichment; the present matrix includes a deliberately bounded introduction.
4. Calibrate lesson length and new-item load with pilot learners.
5. Determine whether first-release speaking/writing evidence is self-reviewed, human-reviewed, automation-assisted, or combined.
6. Establish final assessment duration, alternate-form count, and accommodations.
7. Calibrate mastery weights and passing thresholds using observed delayed retention, not only immediate accuracy.
